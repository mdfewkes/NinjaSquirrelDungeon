# Notes of game lore

## Noah's Summary from late November

The Sewers are all that was left of a bygone, more technologically advanced age.

The rodent families secured the warm shelter of the Sewers generations ago. 

They barricaded and trapped the Sewer, turning it into their fortress, and turning the other woodland creatures away. 

Despite the massive advantage that the Sewer has given the rodents, their attitude of self-serving preservation, has turned in on itself and the three rodent families are constantly fighting amongst one another inside the Sewers, vying for even more control and luxury. 

The rodent families are the Keenclaws (quick, fast, skilled), the Bantombolds (big, brave, British), and the Pawsticks (the smart, administrative, cunning). 

Shortly before our game begins, one of the rodent families stole the magical acorn macguffin that has been the only thing keeping the outdoor woodland creatures from perishing in the harsh elements of their unforgiving world. 

In response, the woodland families deliberated on what to do for too long, and the player character, a squirrel ninja has gone off on their own to get the acorn back, while the leaders scratch their heads. 

Armed only with shurikens (_which let you snuff out candles_), a katana (_which might fling enemies into dungeon hazards_) and packets of powdered broth (_healing item which might require drops of dew to refill_) the player must infiltrate the dungeon, outsmart the many enemies, and retrieve the acorn, to save the day.

[Noah and Amanda brainstorming the healing mechanic](https://discord.com/channels/1372973188425842969/1437168420352163992/1440800786144694384):
- brewing tea instead of just collecting hearts
- might make healing feel more rare and valuable
- they're in a sewer, so clean water is a little iffy
> Does our intrepid Shadowtail have an (functionally) unlimited supply of powdered broth, but they can only brew it at checkpoints? Can they only carry three prepared soups until the next checkpoint? Do they need to find roots and other things to brew with their broth? I wasn't sure how much that might need to be involved so for now I figured it's just nice and flexible as the game gets built out!
> But there are a few water options - little campfires to boil, collecting dew/moisture as you go, that kind of thing
> It would make sense to collect the dew in your little flasks (trying not to borrow too much souls stuff), then when you reach  checkpoint/rest area, you can mix with your broth and other things youv'e found along the way
> So that unlocking the potion was a big crafting job, but once you had it, you just had to keep it topped up

[Mark's followup questions on the healing mechanic](https://discord.com/channels/1372973188425842969/1437168420352163992/1451376957127262368):
> Could we flesh out the "fire" mechanic a little more? Were y'all thinking that would be the only way to heal? Or that it would be more like a cooking mechanic where I can find and use items at any time, but at a "fire" (or trash can or leaf pile) I can combine certain items into other items - e.g. strength/defense buffs or items with stronger healing effects, etc?
> If it's the former, I think it will give the game a higher difficulty and a strong feeling of trying to make it to the next fire before you die. If it's the latter, it raises the complexity a fair bit and the number and variety of items we have to create.
> There's probably a middle of the road option there where fires are your save/respawn points, and you have a small number of healings you can do between fires (similar to Nine Sols, Ender Lilies, etc). So then "brewing tea" at the fire could potentially give you another refillable healing slot (e.g. I can heal 3 times between fires instead of 2 now)
